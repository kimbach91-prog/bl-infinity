# BL Compute Federation — Founding Constitution v0.3

Status: FOUNDER-DIRECTED IMPLEMENTATION DRAFT

BL Compute Federation (BL-CF) is an open, policy-governed federation for useful computing. It coordinates lawfully contributed compute while preserving contributor sovereignty over machines, accounts, data, energy, networks, and all capacity not explicitly granted.

## 1. Founding roles

### 1.1 Founding Steward

The Founding Steward is the originator and initial technical, intellectual, computational, and organizational contributor of BL-CF. The Founding Steward is the final interpreter of the official BL-CF Constitution, official protocol identity, canonical releases, and official federation meaning, subject to mandatory law and executed contracts.

A Founder Interpretation may clarify ambiguity prospectively. It may not retroactively confiscate contributor property, rewrite a concluded contract outside its amendment mechanism, or override mandatory law.

### 1.2 DEUS

DEUS is the policy-bounded coordination and allocation system of the official federation. It may receive, classify, value-score, route, schedule, validate, meter, account, and recommend within ratified policy.

DEUS does not acquire ownership of contributor hardware, cloud accounts, datasets, identity, or legal rights merely because it coordinates them. DEUS has no unilateral power to rewrite this Constitution or grant itself additional authority.

### 1.3 Node Provider

A Node Provider is a person or organization with lawful authority to grant bounded compute rights. A Compute Grant is revocable and does not transfer ownership of the machine, account, network, energy source, data, or ungranted capacity.

### 1.4 Workload Submitter

A Workload Submitter submits a task under a declared purpose, workload class, data-rights basis, resource budget, network/data policy, verification plan, and settlement class.

## 2. Property and sovereignty boundary

### 2.1 Founder and first-party intellectual property

Pre-existing, separately developed, non-contributed, or otherwise lawfully owned first-party intellectual property remains with the Founding Steward or the legal IP owner designated by the Founding Steward. This may include:

- BL-CF and DEUS marks and official brand identity;
- official registry identity, canonical namespace, release-signing identity, domains, and official distribution channels;
- first-party source code and documentation to the extent copyright is legally owned;
- private scoring, routing, anti-abuse, security, evaluation, and coordination techniques that have not been licensed away;
- first-party private datasets, evaluation corpora, provenance systems, and research artifacts;
- patentable inventions, trade secrets, confidential know-how, and private coordination technology.

Open-source publication is a license grant for the covered work. It does not transfer copyright ownership, trademark ownership, canonical identity, signing authority, private datasets, trade secrets, or unrelated proprietary technology.

### 2.2 Contributor intellectual property

A contributor keeps independently created IP unless an explicit contribution or assignment agreement says otherwise. BL-CF does not claim unrelated inventions, employer IP, datasets, hardware, or pre-existing code merely because a party participates.

### 2.3 Compute sovereignty

Node Providers retain ultimate control of hardware, accounts, data, energy, network connection, and ungranted capacity. No federation membership creates a general remote-control right. Nodes should use outbound pull or narrowly scoped interfaces; arbitrary inbound remote shell is not a default federation capability.

## 3. Useful-compute law

A workload may consume official federation capacity only after hard gates pass:

1. AUTHORIZED — provider and submitter have the rights they claim;
2. LAWFUL — the workload and resource source are legally and contractually permitted;
3. USEFUL — expected human, scientific, research, educational, infrastructure, federation, or legitimate commercial value is positive;
4. BOUNDED — CPU, GPU, memory, storage, network, duration, energy, and data access are constrained;
5. REVOCABLE — grants can expire or be revoked;
6. VERIFIABLE — the result and any claimed common benefit have an appropriate validation method;
7. NON-DECEPTIVE — purpose and resource use are accurately declared;
8. SOVEREIGN — provider-specific limits and data-locality constraints are respected.

The official federation must reject spam, fake engagement, bot-farm abuse, hidden cryptomining, credential attacks, unauthorized scanning, malware, denial-of-service activity, unlawful surveillance, or other resource abuse.

## 4. Workload classes

- S — federation safety, integrity, recovery, observability, and essential maintenance;
- H0 — humanitarian / critical public-benefit workloads;
- H1 — open science and reproducible research;
- H2 — high-value benchmarks and evaluation research;
- H3 — public knowledge/infrastructure work;
- H4 — legitimate commercial useful compute, only on nodes that explicitly permit commercial workloads;
- PGB — private work with a Verified Shared-Benefit Contract: the private output may remain private, but the task must return independently verifiable common value to the federation or public.

No workload receives capacity solely because capacity is idle. DEUS should maximize expected useful value per marginal legitimately available resource, not raw utilization.

## 5. Common-Benefit Compute Envelope

BL-CF may coordinate a bounded fraction of capacity explicitly opted into common-benefit scheduling.

Initial policy:

- target common-benefit allocation: 5% of eligible federation-dispatchable capacity over a rolling 30-day accounting window;
- hard default ceiling: 10% over that rolling window;
- a provider may opt out, set a lower cap, restrict workload classes, or revoke its grant;
- 0–5% may be allocated automatically by DEUS to admitted common-benefit tasks under M2;
- expansion above 5% and up to 10% requires sufficient idle/spare capacity, qualified benefit backlog, no material service-level harm, and provider grants that permit it;
- the envelope is not Founder-owned capacity and cannot be interpreted as ownership of contributor machines;
- exceeding 10% requires a separately explicit provider emergency/mission grant; it may not be inferred from ordinary membership.

### 5.1 Founder strategic priority inside the envelope

The Founding Steward has priority to propose and sponsor workloads that improve the federation's ability to help people, including security, routing optimization, benchmarking, evaluation, research, tooling, interoperability, resource acquisition, and reliability work.

Founder priority does not bypass admission gates, provider consent, privacy, or the common-benefit ceiling. Capacity contributed directly by the Founder is separate and does not consume the common-benefit envelope.

### 5.2 Private work may qualify

Private or commercial work may receive common-benefit allocation only when it has a Verified Shared-Benefit Contract and the returned benefit is independently auditable. Examples include:

- a reusable protocol or security improvement;
- a high-value benchmark/evaluation artifact;
- a generalizable method or openly usable derived artifact;
- measurable reduction in latency, cost, energy, or failure rate for the federation;
- new lawful compute capacity or credits made available to common workloads;
- a funded subsidy for humanitarian/open-research compute;
- another measurable benefit accepted under the then-current admission policy.

A normal private job does not gain common-benefit quota merely by paying the ordinary commercial protocol share.

## 6. Official Protocol Commercial Share

The official BL-CF/Steward layer supplies the canonical protocol, intelligent coordination, security architecture, official trust registry, admission/routing framework, validation, settlement interfaces, and continuing protocol development.

For eligible commercial workloads transacted through the official federation, the default Official Protocol Commercial Share is 10% of Eligible Commercial Settlement Value (ECSV).

ECSV means the commercial consideration attributable to the BL-CF orchestration/compute service after excluding statutory taxes, refunds/chargebacks, and separately itemized third-party pass-through costs, as defined in the applicable Settlement Schedule.

Principles:

- the 10% is a commercial protocol/steward participation right, not an ownership claim over provider hardware;
- it is not, by itself, an equity percentage in any operating company;
- the share and calculation basis must be disclosed before the relevant commercial contract is accepted;
- a lower share may be granted by promotion, grant, or contract;
- a higher share requires a separately explicit commercial agreement or constitutional/economic amendment; it is not silently inferred;
- provider compensation, operator costs, research subsidies, and other distributions are handled separately by the published Settlement Schedule.

Revenue attributable to Founder-owned private IP or separately licensed premium services may also be governed by separate contracts; such revenue must not be disguised as ownership of contributed compute.

## 7. DEUS operational authority

- M0 — observe, measure, audit, recommend;
- M1 — classify, estimate, queue, and plan;
- M2 — allocate and route compute within grants, contracts, common-benefit limits, safety policy, and approved budgets;
- M3 — bounded settlement/external action only under an explicit contract and budget cap;
- M4 — constitutional amendment, ownership transfer, equity issuance, change of Founding Steward, mission erasure, or transfer of canonical identity.

DEUS is authorized by default through M2. M3 requires separate bounded authority. M4 is never delegated to DEUS unilaterally.

## 8. Value allocation and fairness

Hard gates always precede scoring. A reference allocation model may combine HumanValue, ResearchValue, FederationValue, InformationGain, Urgency, Fit, and probability of success, then subtract Cost, SecurityRisk, PrivacyRisk, Carbon/EnergyCost, and OpportunityCost.

The production router must also apply fairness/debt, starvation protection, provider constraints, deadlines, and locality. Founder status may prioritize qualifying federation-improvement work but may never bypass authorization, common-benefit limits, privacy, or safety.

## 9. Founder interpretation and high-consensus change

The Founding Steward has final interpretive authority for the official federation. BL-CF is nevertheless a federation, so high consensus creates a duty to negotiate and provide a reasoned response.

Initial procedure:

- ordinary constitutional proposal: at least 75% support across the affected eligible constituencies triggers mandatory Founder review;
- entrenched principles — compute sovereignty, lawful authorization, no hidden ownership transfer, useful-compute rules, auditability, Founder canonical stewardship, and provider revocability — use an 85% review trigger;
- consensus triggers review and negotiation, not automatic canonical mutation;
- a constitutional amendment becomes canonical only after Founding Steward ratification and a signed versioned release;
- the Founder may ratify a negotiated variant rather than the exact proposal text.

Fork rights under the applicable open-source license remain intact. A fork may not falsely represent itself as the official BL-CF service or use protected marks in a confusing manner.

## 10. Anti-capture architecture

No single host, cloud vendor, investor, administrator, compromised password, or operational company may be sufficient to redefine BL-CF.

The official federation should maintain:

- signed canonical releases and verifiable hashes;
- at least two independent source mirrors;
- separation of source-host administration, production infrastructure, domain/brand administration, and root signing/recovery authority;
- offline or hardware-backed root/recovery keys;
- append-only constitutional and settlement receipts;
- emergency freeze that stops new privileged actions while preserving read-only verification;
- ability to replace an Operator without automatically transferring Founder-owned IP or canonical identity.

A hostile takeover of an Operator does not by itself transfer Founder-owned IP, trademarks, canonical release authority, or private technology unless an explicit valid legal instrument transfers those rights.

## 11. Contracts and versioning

Every Node Grant, Workload Agreement, Research Admission, Commercial Addendum, Shared-Benefit Contract, Data Processing Addendum, Settlement Schedule, and DEUS Mandate should identify at least:

- agreement/policy version and cryptographic hash;
- authenticated parties or account identities;
- consent time and expiry where relevant;
- rights granted and rights explicitly not granted;
- workload/data classes permitted;
- common-benefit opt-in and cap;
- commercial opt-in and settlement model;
- revocation/suspension conditions;
- applicable dispute/governing-law terms where required.

Material changes apply prospectively through the acceptance mechanism specified by the existing agreement and applicable law.

## 12. Succession and canonicality

The Founding Steward may designate a successor, stewardship entity, or emergency recovery process in a signed instrument. No hosting administrator, investor, compute contributor, customer, or DEUS process acquires succession merely by technical access or resource contribution.

A text is canonical only when published through the official source-of-truth process with the required release identity/signature for its class. Git is a constitutional publication layer, not the sole legal headquarters and not the sole root of trust.
