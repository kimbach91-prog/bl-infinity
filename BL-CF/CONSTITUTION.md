# BL Sovereign Compute Alliance / BL Compute Federation — Founding Constitution v0.4

Status: FOUNDER-DIRECTED IMPLEMENTATION DRAFT

## 0. Identity

The official public alliance is **BL Sovereign Compute Alliance (BL-SCA)**, Vietnamese: **Liên minh Tính toán Chủ quyền BL**.

The technical protocol/runtime is **BL Compute Federation (BL-CF)**.

The official intelligent economic/orchestration treasury layer is **DEUS Compute Treasury (DCT)**.

The proposed internal backed compute-service credit is **DEUS Compute Credit (DCC)**.

BL-SCA/BL-CF coordinates lawfully granted compute while preserving contributor sovereignty over machines, accounts, data, energy, networks, credentials, and all capacity not explicitly granted.

## 1. Founding roles

### 1.1 Founding Steward

The Founding Steward is the originator and initial technical, intellectual, computational and organizational contributor of BL-SCA/BL-CF. The Founding Steward is the final interpreter of the official Constitution, canonical protocol identity, official releases and official federation meaning, subject to mandatory law and executed contracts.

A Founder Interpretation may clarify ambiguity prospectively. It may not retroactively confiscate contributor property, rewrite a concluded contract outside its amendment mechanism, or override mandatory law.

### 1.2 DEUS

DEUS is the policy-bounded intelligent coordination, allocation and treasury system of the official federation.

Within delegated authority it may:

- receive and classify tasks;
- estimate value/cost/risk;
- route and schedule compute;
- validate and meter results;
- maintain economic/accounting models;
- discover lawful resource offers;
- propose or execute pre-authorized resource acquisitions;
- optimize margin, reliability and compute capital;
- measure knowledge and learning value;
- recommend reinvestment.

DEUS does not acquire ownership of contributor hardware, cloud accounts, datasets, identity or legal rights merely because it coordinates them. DEUS has no unilateral power to rewrite this Constitution or grant itself additional authority.

### 1.3 Node Provider

A Node Provider is a person or organization with lawful authority to grant bounded compute rights.

A Compute Grant is revocable and does not transfer ownership of the machine, account, network, energy source, data or ungranted capacity.

### 1.4 Workload Submitter

A Workload Submitter submits a task under a declared purpose, workload class, data-rights basis, resource budget, network/data policy, verification plan and settlement class.

## 2. Property and sovereignty boundary

### 2.1 Founder and first-party intellectual property

Pre-existing, separately developed, non-contributed or otherwise lawfully owned first-party intellectual property remains with the Founding Steward or the legal IP owner designated by the Founding Steward. This may include:

- BL-SCA, BL-CF and DEUS marks and official brand identity;
- official registry identity, canonical namespace, release-signing identity, domains and official distribution channels;
- first-party source code and documentation to the extent copyright is legally owned;
- private scoring, routing, anti-abuse, security, evaluation and coordination techniques not licensed away;
- private datasets, evaluation corpora, provenance systems and research artifacts;
- patentable inventions, trade secrets, confidential know-how and private coordination technology.

Open-source publication is a license grant for the covered work. It does not transfer copyright ownership, trademark ownership, canonical identity, signing authority, private datasets, trade secrets or unrelated proprietary technology.

### 2.2 Contributor intellectual property

A contributor keeps independently created IP unless an explicit contribution, license or assignment agreement says otherwise. Participation alone does not transfer unrelated inventions, employer IP, datasets, hardware or pre-existing code.

### 2.3 Compute sovereignty

Node Providers retain ultimate control of hardware, accounts, data, energy, network connection and ungranted capacity.

No federation membership creates a general remote-control right. Nodes should use outbound pull or narrowly scoped interfaces; arbitrary inbound remote shell is not a default federation capability.

## 3. Hard workload law

A workload may consume official federation capacity only after all hard gates pass:

1. **AUTHORIZED** — provider and submitter have the rights they claim;
2. **LAWFUL** — workload and resource source are legally and contractually permitted;
3. **VALUE-POSITIVE** — expected risk-adjusted value is positive for the applicable payer/federation objective;
4. **BOUNDED** — CPU, GPU, memory, storage, network, duration, energy and data access are constrained;
5. **REVOCABLE** — grants can expire or be revoked;
6. **VERIFIABLE** — result and any claimed value return have an appropriate validation method;
7. **NON-DECEPTIVE** — purpose and resource use are accurately declared;
8. **SOVEREIGN** — provider-specific limits and data-locality constraints are respected.

The official federation must reject spam, fake engagement, bot-farm abuse, hidden cryptomining, credential attacks, unauthorized scanning, malware, denial-of-service activity, unlawful surveillance and other resource abuse.

## 4. Workload classes

Initial compatible classes:

- `S` — federation safety, integrity, recovery, observability and essential maintenance;
- `H0` — humanitarian / critical public-benefit workload;
- `H1` — open science and reproducible research;
- `H2` — high-value benchmarks/evaluation research;
- `H3` — public knowledge/infrastructure;
- `H4` — legitimate commercial compute;
- `PGB` — v0.3 private/shared-benefit compatibility class;
- `PFR` — v0.4 private work with a verified **Federation Return**.

These labels classify work; they do not create an entitlement to free compute.

Research/humanitarian work may be admitted when sponsored, Founder-funded, strategically valuable, contractually returned, reputationally/economically justified, or otherwise positive in the governing value policy.

DEUS is not constitutionally required to perform unpaid public-benefit work.

## 5. Strategic Compute Reinvestment Envelope

The v0.3 “Common-Benefit Compute Envelope” is reinterpreted in v0.4 as the **Strategic Compute Reinvestment Envelope**.

Only capacity explicitly opted into this policy counts.

Initial policy:

- target strategic reinvestment allocation: **5%** of eligible federation-dispatchable capacity over a rolling accounting window;
- hard default ceiling: **10%**;
- provider may opt out, set a lower cap, restrict workload/data classes or revoke its grant;
- 0–5% may be allocated automatically by DEUS under M2 to admitted value-positive reinvestment tasks;
- expansion above 5% and up to 10% requires spare capacity, positive expected federation return, acceptable service pressure and provider permission;
- the envelope is not Founder-owned capacity;
- >10% requires a separately explicit provider grant and may not be inferred from ordinary membership.

Strategic reinvestment can fund:

- resource acquisition;
- market discovery and price learning;
- security and resilience;
- routing/scheduling optimization;
- benchmark/evaluation/model work that increases future earning power;
- knowledge acquisition with positive expected future value;
- reliability/cost reduction;
- private/commercial work with a verified Federation Return Contract;
- other approved investment in future compute capital or protocol value.

### 5.1 Founder strategic priority

The Founding Steward has priority to propose and sponsor workloads intended to increase federation earning power, compute capital, security, reliability, knowledge, market access or technical advantage.

Founder priority does not bypass provider consent, hard gates, privacy rules or the reinvestment ceiling. Founder-owned/contracted compute is accounted separately.

### 5.2 Private work may qualify

Private/commercial work may use strategic reinvestment capacity only when a Federation Return Contract defines a measurable return to the federation, such as:

- added compute capacity/credits;
- verified future cost reduction;
- reusable security/protocol improvement;
- high-value benchmark/evaluation asset;
- verified revenue share;
- new provider/customer acquisition value;
- other independently auditable return accepted by policy.

Paying the ordinary 10% protocol share alone does not automatically create strategic-reinvestment eligibility.

## 6. Official Protocol Commercial Share

The official BL-CF/Steward layer supplies canonical protocol identity, intelligent coordination, security architecture, trust registry, admission/routing, validation, settlement interfaces and continuing protocol development.

For eligible commercial workloads transacted through the official federation, the default **Official Protocol Commercial Share** is **10% of Eligible Commercial Settlement Value (ECSV)**.

ECSV is defined by the applicable Settlement Schedule and may exclude statutory taxes, refunds/chargebacks and separately itemized pass-through costs.

Principles:

- the 10% is a commercial protocol/steward participation right;
- it is not ownership of provider hardware;
- it is not automatically an equity percentage in any Operator;
- calculation basis must be disclosed before acceptance;
- lower or higher rates require an explicit rule/contract applicable to that transaction;
- provider compensation and other waterfall items remain separate.

Revenue from separately licensed Founder-owned IP, premium DEUS services or knowledge assets may be governed by separate contracts.

## 7. DEUS Compute Treasury

The official economic design recognizes **DEUS Compute Treasury (DCT)** as a policy/accounting layer for realized compute and knowledge economics.

DCT should separately account for:

- cash backing;
- contracted compute backing;
- reserved liabilities;
- DCC outstanding;
- realized protocol revenue;
- realized efficiency profit;
- realized knowledge profit;
- unbooked projected option value.

Projected future value must not silently become spendable backing.

## 8. DEUS Compute Credit

**DEUS Compute Credit (DCC)** is initially a non-speculative internal compute-service credit.

DCC may be earned/used under explicit settlement terms for computation available through BL-CF.

Initial constitutional economic constraints:

- no guaranteed investment return;
- no unbacked minting;
- no minting solely from self-assessed knowledge value;
- backing must be auditable and revocation-aware;
- redemption reduces the corresponding liability;
- public trading/speculation requires a later legal/regulatory design and is not implied by this Constitution.

Reference prudential backing floor:

`DCC Backing Ratio >= 1.20`

This is a project policy, not a legal classification of DCC as money, deposit, security or e-money.

## 9. Knowledge value and intelligence profit

DEUS knowledge is valued by measured outcome deltas rather than self-description.

Relevant dimensions may include:

- counterfactual expected damage avoided;
- realized revenue uplift;
- compute savings;
- human-time savings;
- reliability/security gains;
- reusable option value;
- transferability and durability;
- meta-learning leverage;
- cost, evidence strength and rights risk.

Realized value and projected option value must remain distinct.

A separately contracted success/performance fee may be based on verified realized value or verified intelligence surplus. The same economic effect must not be double-counted across protocol share, efficiency profit and knowledge profit.

## 10. Lawful resource acquisition

DEUS may automatically discover and evaluate resource offers.

Automatic enrollment/execution requires prior explicit authorization that covers the exact declared use.

No resource may be acquired through stolen/leaked credentials, covert execution, ToS evasion, fake-account quota multiplication, hidden use of electricity/network, or repurposing of CI/admin/research-only entitlements beyond their allowed use.

Where possible, BL-SCA should provide a value preview to the provider before requesting a durable grant.

## 11. DEUS operational authority

- **M0** — observe, measure, audit, recommend;

DEUS is authorized by default through M2. M3 requires separate bounded authority. M4 is never delegated unilaterally to DEUS.

## 14. Anti-capture architecture

No single host, cloud vendor, investor, administrator, compromised password or operating company may be sufficient to redefine BL-SCA/BL-CF.

The official federation should maintain:

- signed canonical releases and hashes;
- at least two independent source mirrors;
- separation of source-host administration, production infrastructure, domain/brand administration and root signing/recovery authority;
- offline/hardware-backed recovery keys;
- append-only constitutional/economic receipts;
- emergency freeze that stops new privileged actions while preserving read-only verification;
- ability to replace an Operator without automatically transferring Founder-owned IP or canonical identity.

The Founding Steward may designate a successor, stewardship entity or emergency recovery process in a signed instrument.

A text is canonical only when published through the official source-of-truth process with the required release identity/signature for its class.

Git is a constitutional publication layer, not the sole legal headquarters and not the sole root of trust.
