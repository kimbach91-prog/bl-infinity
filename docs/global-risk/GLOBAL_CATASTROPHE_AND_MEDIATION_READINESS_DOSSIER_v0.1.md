# Global Catastrophe & Mediation Readiness Dossier v0.1

Status: PREPAREDNESS ONLY / NON-CANONICAL / NO CLAIM OF MANDATE
Date: 2026-09-07

## 1. Purpose

Prepare a lawful, neutral and technically credible package that can support governments, international organizations, humanitarian actors and affected communities during major cross-border crises.

This dossier does **not** claim authority to mediate. Mediation may be offered only when the relevant parties consent or an appropriate lawful mandate exists.

## 2. Readiness gates before offering mediation

All gates must pass:

1. **Consent / mandate** — the relevant parties or a competent institution accept the role.
2. **Independence** — no undisclosed financial, intelligence, military or commercial conflict that materially compromises neutrality.
3. **Impartial process** — all sides receive equivalent procedural safeguards even when their conduct is not morally or legally equivalent.
4. **Technical competence** — verified experts exist for the subject matter actually in dispute.
5. **Confidentiality** — secure channels, retention rules and information-sharing boundaries are operational.
6. **Humanitarian floor** — civilian protection and international humanitarian/human-rights obligations are not traded away.
7. **Verification capacity** — ceasefires, resource flows and technical claims can be independently checked.
8. **Funding transparency** — material funders and conditions are disclosed to participants.
9. **No coercive dependency** — compute/technology services are never conditioned on political obedience.
10. **Exit / oversight** — mandate, term, complaint path and termination mechanism are explicit.

Compute capacity alone never satisfies these gates.

No outreach package should imply endorsement before endorsement exists.

## 4. Standard crisis dossier

For each crisis maintain:

### A. Verified situation picture

- event timeline;
- actors and formal authorities;
- geographic scope;
- confirmed vs disputed facts;
- civilian/humanitarian conditions;
- critical infrastructure state;
- nuclear/biological/chemical or other catastrophic hazards;
- food/water/energy exposure;
- displacement;
- communication failures;
- source provenance and confidence.

### B. Interest and constraint map

For each principal actor:

- stated objectives;
- likely minimum acceptable outcomes;
- public and private constraints where lawfully known;
- red lines;
- domestic legitimacy constraints;
- security concerns;
- economic/resource dependencies;
- humanitarian obligations;
- face-saving options;
- issues that can be separated from sovereignty disputes.

Never infer secret intent as fact.

### C. Escalation graph

Map causal chains such as:

`incident -> retaliation -> alliance obligation -> infrastructure disruption -> civilian harm -> political pressure -> further escalation`

For every edge record:

- evidence;
- probability band;
- reversibility;
- lead time;
- possible interrupt mechanism.

### D. Immediate catastrophe-prevention package

Before seeking a comprehensive political settlement, test narrow measures such as:

- military hotlines;
- deconfliction channels;
- advance notification of high-risk activity;
- protected repair windows for nuclear/electricity/water infrastructure;
- medical evacuation and humanitarian access;
- prisoner/detainee information exchange where lawful;
- mutually accepted monitoring;
- nuclear/radiological safety arrangements;
- shipping/aviation safety notices;
- no-strike arrangements for specifically protected civilian infrastructure where legally appropriate.

### E. Resource settlement module

Separate sovereignty from operational access where possible.

For water, energy, ports, corridors or shared infrastructure define independently:

- ownership/sovereignty position;
- access rights;
- minimum guaranteed flows/capacity;
- pricing;
- metering;
- emergency allocation;
- maintenance;
- environmental floor;
- dispute resolution;
- periodic review.

### F. Governance / power module

Any transitional structure must define:

- lawful basis;
- composition and selection;
- narrow mandate;
- term and sunset;
- veto scope if any;
- public accountability;
- judicial/review path;
- transfer back to ordinary institutions.

No permanent external trusteeship is assumed.

### G. Religion / ethnicity / identity module

- prohibit collective blame;
- protect worship, language, culture and community life;
- include affected communities in decisions concerning them;
- prevent forced demographic change;
- preserve individual citizenship/equality rights;
- use quotas or communal vetoes only when locally accepted, narrowly designed and periodically reviewed;
- prevent identity representation from becoming permanent elite capture.

## 5. Quiet preparedness boundary

Permitted preparation before a mandate exists:

- open-source research;
- expert rosters;
- scenario modelling;
- legal analysis;
- template agreements;
- humanitarian logistics planning;
- secure communications design;
- translation resources;
- independent verification tooling;
- disaster supply-chain planning;
- stakeholder contact directory from lawful public/consensual sources.

Not permitted under this project:

- covert political influence;
- impersonation;
- bribery or inducement of officials;
- clandestine intelligence collection;
- election manipulation;
- support for coups or regime change;
- unauthorized surveillance;
- interference with military or critical infrastructure;
- false claims of UN, state or humanitarian mandate.

## 6. Technical support BLI-SCF may provide

When requested and authorized, the compute platform can support:

- multi-source evidence reconciliation;
- resource and logistics optimization;
- humanitarian demand forecasts;
- infrastructure failure simulation;
- negotiated-option simulation;
- translation and document comparison;
- treaty/contract consistency checks;
- energy/water allocation scenarios;
- ceasefire monitoring analytics using authorized data;
- provenance-preserving situation reports.

All outputs remain advisory. Human/legal authorities retain decisions.

## 7. First outreach packet — prepared, not sent

A future outreach packet should contain:

1. identity and legal entity information;
2. conflict-of-interest and funding disclosure;
3. BLI-SCF technical capability statement;
4. security/data-sovereignty architecture;
5. catastrophic-use restrictions;
6. audit and governance model;
7. demonstrated pilots / independently verified results;
8. proposed narrowly scoped service;
9. statement that no mandate or endorsement is claimed;
10. contact and due-diligence process.

Do not send until the capability, legal entity, governance and evidence are mature enough for external scrutiny.

## 8. Maturity threshold

Before approaching international organizations as a potential mediation/technical-support partner, target at minimum:

- independently reproducible platform deployment;
- published security model and threat model;
- audited data-governance controls;
- independent technical reviewers;
- documented lawful corporate/non-profit or research vehicle as appropriate;
- transparent funding and conflict disclosures;
- successful non-political disaster/enterprise pilots;
- incident response and liability model;
- multilingual capability;
- clear evidence that safety policies are enforced in runtime, not merely written.
