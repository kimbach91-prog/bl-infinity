# Safe Technology Transfer & Resource Partnership Gate v0.1

Status: PROPOSAL / NON-CANONICAL
Date: 2026-09-07

## Purpose

Enable lawful transfer of BLI-SCF technology to governments, enterprises, universities and infrastructure operators while preserving recipient autonomy and preventing catastrophic, coercive or unlawful use.

This policy applies to sales, licenses, source-code transfer, managed services, joint ventures, compute-for-resource agreements and technology-for-resource agreements.

## 1. Non-negotiable principles

1. **Sovereign autonomy:** recipient controls its own lawful deployment; no undisclosed backdoor, political kill switch or hidden administrative account.
2. **Explicit safety boundaries:** prohibited uses are contractual and technically enforced where feasible.
3. **No coercive barter:** technology is not exchanged for secret political concessions, military alignment, electoral influence or waiver of fundamental rights.
4. **Transparent valuation:** compute, technology, energy, land, connectivity, facilities, minerals and other consideration are independently valued.
5. **Local benefit:** major projects must include local workforce, training, environmental obligations and fair public/private benefit sharing.
6. **Finite contracts:** rights, access and resource commitments have term, review, termination and dispute-resolution clauses.
7. **Auditability:** material transactions and high-risk deployments have auditable records, beneficial-owner checks and anti-corruption controls.
8. **No catastrophic enablement:** a deal that materially increases catastrophic-risk capability fails even when economically attractive.

## 2. Transfer classes

### Class A — Preferred / low risk

Examples:

- grid and energy optimization;
- climate and disaster modelling;
- agriculture and water management;
- public administration and document intelligence;
- education and scientific research;
- enterprise planning;
- logistics and supply-chain resilience;
- non-invasive healthcare support under appropriate regulation.

Default: transferable after standard legal/security/data review.

### Class B — Controlled / regulated

Examples:

- financial infrastructure;
- critical-infrastructure operations;
- sensitive government data;
- large-scale identity systems;
- advanced cybersecurity defence;
- high-capacity biological/chemical research support with legitimate civilian purpose.

Default: enhanced due diligence, deployment boundaries, logging and independent review.

### Class C — High-risk dual use

Examples:

- frontier compute clusters with broad model-development capability;
- tools capable of materially accelerating weapons-relevant research;
- autonomous control of safety-critical physical systems;
- advanced cyber capabilities that can cross from defence to destructive operations.

Default: no transfer without named legal authority, independent catastrophic-risk review, strict end-use scope, monitoring and renewal gates.

### Class D — Prohibited

Do not knowingly provide technology, tuning, workflows or compute for:

- nuclear, chemical or biological weapons development;
- mass-casualty targeting or optimization;
- fully autonomous lethal target selection/engagement without meaningful lawful human control;
- destructive offensive cyber operations against civilian critical infrastructure;
- unlawful mass surveillance, political repression or persecution by protected identity;
- manipulation of elections or political processes through covert influence;
- torture, enforced disappearance or collective punishment;
- deliberate creation or amplification of pandemic-capable pathogens;
- evasion of sanctions/export controls where applicable.

## 3. Ten-gate transaction review

A transfer proceeds only after all applicable gates pass:

1. **Authority Gate** — identify contracting entity, legal authority and decision-maker.
2. **Ownership Gate** — beneficial ownership, conflicts, corruption and sanctions/export-control screening.
3. **Purpose Gate** — documented intended use and expected public/economic benefit.
4. **Capability Gate** — what new capability does the technology actually unlock?
5. **Catastrophic-Risk Gate** — could misuse cause mass casualties, strategic instability or loss of critical control?
6. **Data Gate** — ownership, residency, privacy, retention and access rights.
7. **Deployment Gate** — technical boundaries, operator roles, network exposure and credential model.
8. **Audit Gate** — logs, independent inspection, incident reporting and evidence retention.
9. **Suspension/Remedy Gate** — explicit contractual remedies for material misuse; no secret remote control.
10. **Renewal Gate** — periodic re-approval for high-risk capability rather than perpetual escalation rights.

## 4. Technology-for-resource partnership

Acceptable consideration may include:

- electricity or long-term power capacity;
- renewable generation/storage capacity;
- data-centre land or facilities;
- fibre/connectivity;
- sovereign compute capacity;
- lawful equipment supply;
- research facilities and talent programs;
- lawful mineral/material supply under independent environmental and labour review;
- cash or standard commercial consideration.

### Required contract structure

- independent market valuation of both technology and resource consideration;
- published or auditable beneficial ownership;
- anti-bribery and conflict-of-interest clauses;
- resource quantity/quality measurement by agreed independent method;
- environmental and community-impact floor;
- local workforce/training obligations for material infrastructure projects;
- no transfer of political or territorial authority;
- no exclusive control of essential public infrastructure unless explicitly lawful and independently reviewed;
- termination, force-majeure and dispute-resolution mechanisms;
- source-code escrow or reproducible-build pathway when sovereign operational continuity requires it;
- clear ownership of improvements, models and locally generated data.

## 5. Sovereignty-safe technical design

For sovereign deployments:

- credentials belong to the recipient, not the vendor;
- cryptographic keys are locally controlled;
- update channels are transparent and pin-able;
- source/binaries are reproducible where contractually required;
- telemetry is opt-in and purpose-limited;
- recipient can operate in disconnected mode when feasible;
- no dependency on a single external model provider;
- no private reasoning or cross-tenant data is exported;
- no hidden remote shutdown facility.

If a safety suspension mechanism is required for a high-risk managed service, it must be explicit in contract, technically documented, narrow in scope and subject to agreed due process.

## 6. Monitoring without surveillance

Monitor the service, not the population.

Permitted examples:

- compute consumption;
- model/version provenance;
- safety-policy violations;
- system/network integrity;
- declared application class;
- anomalous attempts to bypass capability gates.

Not permitted by default:

- unrelated citizen communications;
- political beliefs or affiliations;
- indiscriminate biometric tracking;
- content unrelated to service safety/security.

## 7. Deal decision rule

A transaction is approved only when:

`lawful_authority AND fair_exchange AND recipient_autonomy AND acceptable_externality AND catastrophic_risk_below_threshold`

Economic value cannot average away a failed catastrophic-risk or sovereignty gate.

## 8. Required dossier for each counterparty

Maintain a dated dossier containing:

- entity and jurisdiction;
- beneficial owners / controlling bodies;
- contracting authority;
- intended use;
- technical deployment diagram;
- transfer class;
- end-user/end-use findings;
- sanctions/export-control review where applicable;
- data/residency requirements;
- human-rights and catastrophic-risk assessment;
- resource consideration and independent valuation;
- environmental/community review;
- incident-response contacts;
- audit rights;
- termination/remedy clauses;
- renewal date;
- unresolved red flags.

No dossier marked `UNRESOLVED_CRITICAL` may proceed to transfer.
