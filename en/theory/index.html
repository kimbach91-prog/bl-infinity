ORIGINAL THESIS · PUBLIC INTERPRETATION

BL∞: The reach of possibility

On futures that may become reachable, the limits of our present imagination, and the responsibility to test the ideas we hold.

The starting question is simple to ask, but difficult to close: must the possible future be limited to what people can imagine today? A possibility not yet on our map is not thereby proven to be impossible. I use BL∞ as a name for this open inquiry.

Previously published definition: B∞ encompasses the reachable domains of the future, and the worlds that may lie within, emerge from, or exceed the domains we can currently imagine.

1. What is reachable is not necessarily what has been imagined

When we speak about what might happen, we tend to picture familiar pathways. Those pathways depend on our knowledge, available tools and immediate circumstances. Something outside our current means of observation or description may also fall outside the future we can presently sketch. Every image of the future is therefore conditional.

But the word reachable is not a promise that everything we can imagine can be realized. A wish, a hypothesis and a demonstrated possibility are different states. What I want to preserve is the freedom to search beyond current assumptions, not a license to deny physical or logical constraints or to dispense with evidence.

2. A useful map is still not the world

Human beings need models. We name things, classify phenomena, draw maps, develop equations and propose theories. These tools allow experience to travel across people and generations. Yet each model retains some relationships while leaving others out. Forgetting what has been omitted invites us to confuse the representation of a thing with the thing itself.

I explore this difficulty in the public book Symbols and Essence, originally published in Vietnamese. BL∞ extends the same concern to a wider question: if our present map cannot exhaust reality, how should we organize inquiry so that a better observation can revise the map?

3. No theory should exempt itself from criticism

A shared name helps people focus on a question. Yet once the name becomes a reason not to investigate, it turns into an obstacle. BL∞ should not be read as a guarantee that any individual or system possesses unlimited ability. Testable claims need testable conditions. Claims not yet testable should be described as conjectures or unanswered questions.

In the public writings on Academic Freedom and Academic Democracy, the emphasis is on freedom to ask and criticize, alongside unequal evidential weight. I want to preserve that discipline here. Authors should explain their claims; readers may challenge them; reality owes no allegiance to our chosen terminology.

4. From accumulated knowledge to usable capability

Knowledge becomes practically valuable when it is applied within the conditions that make it reliable. This requires more than an individual knowing a great deal. It also requires ways to organize memory, tools, specialist judgment and decisions. A learning process can improve if errors are retained as lessons, outcomes are compared with expectations, and participants can revise their methods.

The essay Architect of Intelligence develops one aspect of this question. It belongs to the same public line of inquiry, but it is not evidence that an unlimited self-improving system has been built. Between a promising idea and a verified capability lies the work of testing it in the real world.

5. UNKNOWN is a meaningful state

Some questions cannot yet be answered with the tools or evidence available to us. Lacking an answer now does not prove that an answer will remain forever impossible. Equally, it does not authorize us to fill the gap with whatever conclusion we prefer. The final boundary of BL∞ remains UNKNOWN, because the scope of what may ultimately become reachable is not established by the evidence currently available.

UNKNOWN is not a mystical resolution used to end debate. It is a way of keeping the inquiry accountable: what has been observed, what has been inferred, what remains uncertain, and what evidence could change our understanding?

6. An invitation to read and challenge

This public page presents the philosophical starting point, the author's lineage and connections to already published work. It does not stand in for a scientific publication or a complete experimental research record. If a claim is too broad, locate its boundary. If an interpretation is mistaken, show the counterevidence. A better formulation should be welcomed because the purpose is clearer understanding, not the defense of a title.

Further research may be transformed into public essays or reviewable documents when it can be responsibly explained and assessed. Until then, its nonpublication is not evidence of its correctness, value or performance.

7. Scientific and mathematical connections worth studying

Placing a philosophical proposal beside recognized scientific work does not make the proposal scientifically established. The connections here are useful because they sharpen questions about information, uncertainty, possible pathways and the design of learning systems, not because they prove BL∞.

Shannon's information theory offers a measure of uncertainty for a given probability distribution. Bayesian reasoning provides a formal way to update a probability when new evidence arrives, assuming the starting probabilities and likelihoods are defensible. Herbert Simon studied designed systems as proper scientific objects, and Edwin Hutchins showed how cognitive work can be distributed among people, instruments and shared procedures.

Douglas Engelbart proposed an integrated approach to augmenting human intellectual capability. Karl Popper's account of falsifiability, including the important criticisms of that account, points to the need for empirical claims to admit the possibility of being wrong. These works illuminate parts of the BL∞ research question. They do not establish an infinite set of physically attainable future states.

The mathematical operators mentioned on this page, including graph reachability, Bayesian updating and Shannon entropy, belong to established public mathematics. They are not the original BL∞ operators, nor are they reconstructions of unpublished work.

8. Reproducible preliminary illustrations

Before discussing a space of possibilities without a known final boundary, it helps to inspect small models whose assumptions are explicit. The following examples are new public teaching calculations prepared for this edition. Their numerical values have not been authenticated as historical equations from the original BL∞ research records.

A. Finite branching. Suppose each step offers exactly three independent choices. At depth four, a complete tree contains 3⁴ = 81 sequences of length four. Including the root and all intermediate nodes, the count is 1 + 3 + 9 + 27 + 81 = 121.

If different sequences reach the same outcome, fewer distinct states exist than paths. If a path violates physical constraints or available resources, it may not be feasible at all. A count of imagined branches is not a count of states that reality makes reachable.

B. Graph reachability. Consider a directed graph with edges A → B and B → C. A can reach C in two steps. For its adjacency matrix M, the entry (A,C) in M² is 1. This is an ordinary graph-theoretic calculation, not evidence that every named state in an unrestricted possibility space can be attained.

Changing the graph's connections changes the set of reachable states, even if the number of nodes remains the same. The interesting question concerns representations, constraints and valid paths.

C. Updating belief with evidence. Suppose the prior probability of a hypothesis is 20%. A hypothetical positive observation occurs with probability 80% if the hypothesis is true and 10% if it is false. Bayes' theorem yields P(H | E) = (0.8 × 0.2) / [(0.8 × 0.2) + (0.1 × 0.8)] = 2/3 ≈ 66.7%.

The result depends entirely on the chosen inputs and is not a measured probability of BL∞. As another illustration, Shannon entropy for two equally likely alternatives is one bit. For probabilities 2/3 and 1/3, it is about 0.918 bits. These figures describe hypothetical probability distributions, not the power or truth of a philosophical theory.

These elementary exercises keep three notions apart: a path we can describe, a path the system can actually execute, and a belief sufficiently supported by evidence. BL∞ asks a larger question, so the examples are methodological aids rather than proofs.

9. From philosophical proposals to empirical questions

A broad question can generate narrower tests. Within a fixed task set, does adding a tool increase the number of valid solutions people find? If a problem is represented differently, can participants discover additional paths under the same constraints? Across repeated tasks, does a procedure that preserves failed hypotheses and corrective evidence improve decisions compared with one that does not?

These questions can be approached with baseline measurements, controls, comparable resource limits and independent evaluation. Favorable results would support the particular tested claims. They would not establish that all futures are limitless, that everything currently unimagined is physically possible, or that an AI system has attained unbounded intelligence.

Before advancing a public claim to a stronger evidence status, the work should specify its operational definition, scope, test protocol, independently checkable outcomes, negative results, revision history and limitations.

10. Future release gateway for original research

The BL∞ original operators, distinctive mathematical construction and unpublished mechanisms remain WITHHELD. A passing benchmark never automatically authorizes disclosure of source code or formulas.
