# NOVEL PACING CANON — AUTHOR TIME-SKIP LOCK

**Status:** ACTIVE EDITORIAL CANON  
**Effective:** 2026-09-04  
**Authority:** Author — Lâm Kim Bách

## Core rule

The novel does not advance through biography-summary by default.

Narrative time advances at scene scale:

`beat / seconds / minutes -> hours -> day -> author-selected event`

A scene may slow down further when the author wants an event, gesture, sentence, sound, decision or relationship to carry more weight.

## Time-skip lock

Any jump that skips over meaningful lived time — especially multi-day, month, year, childhood-stage or life-stage jumps — requires explicit author permission.

Without author permission:

- do not write `mấy năm sau`, `những mùa hè trôi qua`, `dần dần lớn lên`, or equivalent montage as a bridge;
- do not summarize a long childhood span in one paragraph;
- do not compress multiple formative events into a checklist;
- do not infer which event deserves emphasis merely because research material exists;
- do not use worldbuilding research as a reason to accelerate chronology.

If the next authorized time point is unknown, stop the scene or hold the continuation. Do not invent the jump.

## Structural preservation

Chapter 1 improves around the original structural spine rather than continually replacing it:

1. Grand Ending / terminal countdown;
2. final transfer threshold;
3. cut into childhood;
4. one lived childhood scene at close temporal resolution;
5. stop before the next substantial time jump unless the author has authorized it.

`ImproveStructure != ReplaceStructure`

## Event-intelligence preservation gate

Compression may shorten exposition, but it must not silently delete an event that carries a distinct reasoning mechanism, causal lesson, relationship shift, or future pattern.

Before removing an older event, classify it:

`EVENT -> mechanism -> consequence -> later echo -> keep / move / merge / delete-with-rationale`

High-value events should normally be **moved or merged inside the authorized scene**, not erased merely because a new draft is shorter. Examples of preserved event classes include:

- evidence discipline under pressure;
- collective-intelligence failure despite high individual intelligence;
- KPI/local-optimization failure;
- AI correct-without-explanation vs persuasive-but-wrong;
- structured UNKNOWN preservation;
- resource contribution by multiple intelligence/civilization types;
- anti-savior / competent-worker behavior;
- ambiguous-number coincidence explicitly fenced from causality.

`Shorter != Deeper` and `MoreEvents != Better`; causal leverage decides.

## Paragraph-flow contract

The default prose unit is a **multi-sentence paragraph**, not a sentence card.

Use 2–5 sentences when one physical/emotional/causal unit can stay together. A paragraph should preferably carry at least two of:

- body/location;
- sensory texture;
- character intent;
- pressure or obstacle;
- information update;
- choice;
- observable consequence.

A speaker change may start a new paragraph when clarity benefits, but a two-word reply does not require a blank line on both sides if action can anchor it naturally.

One-line paragraphs are scarce impact tools. They are reserved for:

- earned aura beat;
- irreversible decision;
- shock/reversal;
- clean scene termination.

If three consecutive paragraphs contain only one sentence without a deliberate escalation pattern, treat it as a fragmentation smell.

## Authorial voltage — “ngầu” without power-fantasy prose

Coolness is not declared. It is generated by:

`competence under pressure + restraint + dry wit + clear cost + consequence`

Bách should feel formidable because he notices a distinction others miss, refuses a convenient lie, chooses under cost, or says less than the room expects — not because narrator adjectives announce genius, destiny, superiority or invincibility.

A hard line should normally do at least two jobs: reveal character, compress a doctrine, shift dialogue power, create image, or foreshadow a later pattern. Do not stack aphorisms simply because each line sounds quotable.

`AuraDensity << SceneDensity`

## Sci-fi worldbuilding contract

Worldbuilding enters through **objects, constraints, procedures, failures and reactions** before explanation.

Prefer:

`thing in room -> character interacts -> system resists -> consequence -> reader infers rule`

over:
`narrator explains technology -> scene resumes`.

Technical language may be used when it is the character's working vocabulary and the surrounding action gives the reader enough orientation. A term should not exist only to sound futuristic.

## Dialogue contract

Every recurring speaker has a distinct pressure, vocabulary and rhythm. Dialogue must change at least one state:

- knowledge;
- relationship;
- option set;
- power balance;
- emotional pressure;
- decision readiness.

Banter is allowed when it reveals relationship or character under pressure. Generic cleverness that could be spoken by anyone is cut.

## Serial craft guardrails

The project may learn from successful web-serial craft at the level of **general technique**, never imitate a specific living author's voice.

Research-backed guardrails:

- distinct character voices and consequential interactions can make slow scenes feel substantial;
- slow burn fails when clock-time expands without meaningful state change;
- consistency benefits from a retained backlog/scene map and event ledger;
- serial momentum needs chapter-level payoff, not necessarily artificial cliffhangers;
- excessive exposition, self-monologue and fragmentary micro-chapters can create skim pressure even when worldbuilding is strong.

The desired synthesis for this novel is:

`deep scene + event consequence + distinctive dialogue + restrained exposition + retained mystery`.

## Event dilation

Author-selected events may be expanded far beyond clock duration. A few seconds can occupy pages if the event matters. An ordinary day may receive an entire chapter. Compression is not the default measure of quality.

`NarrativeImportance -> TimeDilationAllowed`

## Childhood pacing

Childhood is not a résumé.

Default unit:

- one room;
- one walk;
- one meal;
- one school period;
- one game;
- one argument;
- one afternoon;
- one day.

Only the author decides which event becomes a bridge to another day or another age.

## Public privacy boundary

The research/private evidence layer may retain precise continuity data, but public prose should disclose only what the scene needs.

By default, public prose must avoid:

- exact home address or route geometry;
- unnecessary school/point-of-interest identifiers;
- real-person mapping behind literary names;
- unnecessary family/private chronology;
- precise dates or ages not required by the scene;
- dense biographical fact dumps.

Use coarse locality, literary names and scene-relevant detail unless the author explicitly releases more.

## Reader-flow boundary

The chapter is prose first. Research notes, provenance tables, historical audits and technical worldbuilding belong in companion material, not at the bottom of the narrative chapter.

`Story != ResearchAppendix`

## Failure conditions

A future edit fails this canon if it:

- makes childhood feel fast-forwarded by an editor rather than lived by the character;
- replaces causal events with a shorter synopsis;
- creates “coolness” mainly by stacked one-line quotes;
- lets narrator explain character greatness instead of making the character earn it in action;
- fragments normal description into sentence cards;
- restores older high-value events by reintroducing an unauthorized time-skip.
